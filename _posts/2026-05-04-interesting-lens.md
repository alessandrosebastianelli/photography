Recently, social media kept showing me ultra-thin lenses called 'pancake lenses' — no, not the breakfast kind! The price was so low I figured, 'worth a shot.' Skeptical (but curious), I bought one to see if all the hype was justified.

Well, color me surprised. This post exists to share this little discovery with you.

And yes, the cover image has been taken with this lens!

## The lens

The AstrHori 10mm F8 II is a fisheye pancake lens for Fujifilm X-Mount cameras. "Pancake" refers to its remarkably flat profile (just about 12mm thick) which makes it almost disappear on the front of your camera body.

The specs are minimal by design: fixed aperture of f/8, manual focus only, and a field of view of 180°. On an APS-C sensor, the 10mm focal length gives a 15mm equivalent, with that characteristic fisheye distortion that curves the world into something almost spherical. The optical formula — 5 elements in 4 groups, including 3 ED (Extra-low Dispersion) elements — is modest but surprisingly capable at keeping chromatic aberration in check.

The body is all-metal, weighing just 130g, and accepts 55mm filters (a UV filter is included). There's no electronic communication with the camera, so you'll need to enable "shoot without lens" in your camera settings.

The fixed f/8 aperture is the most polarizing aspect. It demands good light — or a willingness to push ISO. In return, depth of field is essentially infinite, which turns manual focus into a non-issue: set it to infinity and almost everything is sharp.

At €79, it occupies a strange, delightful niche: part creative toy, part legitimate wide-angle tool.

You can buy the lens on Amazon (https://amzn.eu/d/0ceIzoCy).

## My experience

Well, what can I say — an ultra compact and lightweight lens with interesting results. Don't expect optics comparable to top-tier lenses, but for the price, I really recommend it! And trust me, I wasn't paid to promote it.

Simply put, it's hard to find such a wide-angle, compact lens at such an appealing price.

I'm not a professional photographer, just an enthusiast, so I can't explain all the technical and optical pros and cons, but this lens is always with me. Whether it's landscapes, portraits, or just experimentation, I enjoy using it to try out some more "artistic" or "different" shots — who knows, but it really is a lot of fun.
